Emmanuel Brochet

Montagne de Reims · Villers-aux-Nœuds
The Estate
Appellation Champagne
Grapes Pinot Meunier, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay
Surface 2.5 ha
Production7,000 bottles
Farming Certified Organic

When you meet Emmanuel Brochet, you quickly realize he chose the right path. In 1997, he became a first-generation Champagne grower, working his 2.5ha vineyard on the lieu-dit Le Mont Benoit in Villers-aux-Nœuds. The single vineyard is planted on a slope divided in two — upper vines from 1962, lower from 1984 — on silt, clay and chalk soils.

The approach is entirely artisanal. Nearly all work is done by hand, and Emmanuel has farmed without chemicals for over a decade, receiving organic certification in 2011. In the cellar, a traditional vertical press is used; only the cuvée is selected and brought to barrique for natural fermentation. The wines are pure, precise and mineral — combining ripeness with great freshness.

Vouette & Sorbée

Côte des Bar · Buxières-sur-Arce
The Estate
Appellation Champagne
Grapes Pinot Noir, Chardonnay
Surface 4.5 ha
Farming Certified Biodynamic (Demeter)

Bertrand Gautherot converted his family estate to biodynamic farming in 2001, making Vouette & Sorbée one of the pioneering domaines of the grower Champagne movement. His vineyards sit on Kimmeridgian limestone — the same geology as Chablis — a hundred kilometers south of the classic Champagne zones.

Dosages are zero or near-zero. Fermentations are entirely natural. The wines are uncompromising, electric and tensely mineral — among the finest expressions of what grower Champagne can be when the winemaker gets out of the way and lets the terroir speak.

Laurent Bénard

Côte des Bar · Les Riceys
The Estate
Appellation Champagne
Grapes Pinot Noir, Chardonnay
Surface 4 ha
Farming Organic

Laurent Bénard works his small estate in the Côte des Bar, the southernmost and most underappreciated corner of Champagne. Here, Pinot Noir dominates the Kimmeridgian clay and limestone soils — the same geology that makes Chablis and Sancerre great.

Laurent farms organically and makes wines of remarkable depth and savory minerality. More vinous and structured than much of the region, these Champagnes reward time at the table.

Valentin Zusslin

Orschwihr
The Estate
Appellation Alsace Grand Cru Pfingstberg
Grapes Riesling, Pinot Gris, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Noir
Surface 12 ha
Farming Certified Biodynamic (Demeter)

The Zusslin family has been growing wine in the southern Alsace village of Orschwihr since 1691. Today, Marie and Jean-Paul Zusslin run the estate, biodynamically farmed since 1997 and certified by Demeter since 2002.

Their vineyards lie on the Grand Cru Pfingstberg, a south-facing slope of granite and limestone. In the cellar: minimal intervention, long slow fermentations, extended lees aging, no fining or filtration. Pure expressions of Alsatian terroir.

Domaine des Frères

Chinon · Beaumont-en-Véron
The Estate
Appellation Chinon, Vin de France
Grapes Cabernet Franc, Chenin Blanc
Surface 11 ha
Production45,000 bottles
Farming Organic

Henri and Valentin Bruneau grew up near Chinon and both became engineers before realizing the office was not for them. Henri returned to study viticulture and oenology in 2013; Valentin followed in 2015. In October 2019 they took over a unique estate of 27 acres of vines between 35 and 50 years old on a hillside containing 7 distinct terroirs, with a cellar cut into the local tuffeau limestone.

Farming is organic with precise tisanes, harvest by hand before dawn. In just a few harvests the brothers have built a quiet reputation in France for wines that are pure, crunchy and deeply expressive of their place.

The Estate

Emmanuel Brochet

Montagne de Reims · Villers-aux-Nœuds
The Estate
AppellationChampagne
GrapesPinot Meunier, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay
Surface2.5 ha
Production7,000 bottles
FarmingCertified Organic

When you meet Emmanuel Brochet, you quickly realize he chose the right path. In 1997, he became a first-generation Champagne grower, working his 2.5ha vineyard on the lieu-dit Le Mont Benoit in Villers-aux-Nœuds. The single vineyard is planted on a slope divided in two — upper vines from 1962, lower from 1984 — on silt, clay and chalk soils.

The approach is entirely artisanal. Nearly all work is done by hand, and Emmanuel has farmed without chemicals for over a decade, receiving organic certification in 2011. In the cellar, a traditional vertical press is used; only the cuvée is selected and brought to barrique for natural fermentation. The wines are pure, precise and mineral — combining ripeness with great freshness.
